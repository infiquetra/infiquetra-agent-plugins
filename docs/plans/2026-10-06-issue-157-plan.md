# Policy questions and question banks for the four lenses (issue #157)

## Summary

Saga publishes the policy's 30 questions as one JSON list that builders declare against, plus four Jev question banks (security, correctness, testing, architecture and maintainability) of 10 to 20 questions each for the sweep to ask. Each lens gets one sitting with the operator, the approval is fingerprinted into the bank file, and the loader refuses any set whose content drifted from its approval.

---

## Problem Frame

This is card C7 of the saga review redesign (parent issue #147). Issue #157 is requirements-ready. What a lens checks today lives as prose dimensions in the lifecycle repository's lens catalogue, the only review question Jev asks is the five-question `lenses` verb over a whole diff, and the eleven optional lenses are picked per run by planner declaration.

The formats this card builds on have landed: C6's sweep and bank schema (issue #156), C2's calibration file and fingerprint list (issue #149), and the C4a tool framework plus C4b's Python, CDK, shell, workflow and Markdown adapters (issues #151 and #152). Not yet written are C4c's remaining language adapters, C5's pattern checks and C5b's scripted checks, which the sittings must review, so the sittings are this plan's last unit and wait for those three cards.

The policy draft itself sits in the operator's private notes, which this repository must never quote. The implementer drafts all wording from public sources only: the lens tables and named question topics in the design plan, this repository's public history, and freshly written examples. The operator, who holds the draft, corrects the wording at the sittings, which is what makes the sittings load-bearing rather than ceremonial.

Two landed facts shape the file design. The sweep's bank readers take only `schema` and `questions` from the bank object and ignore any other top-level key, while per-question keys are closed to exactly the eight C6 names. So the approval block, the lens tag and the policy links ride at the top level of each bank file, and the folded source lens rides inside the question id, which is the one per-question field that travels all the way into sweep items and verdict logs.

## Requirements

Requirements are grouped by concern. R-IDs run continuously. Each one traces to the issue's acceptance criteria, numbered AC-1 through AC-9 in the order the issue lists them.

**The policy list**

- R1. `plugins/saga/references/question-banks/policy-questions.json` holds the policy's 30 questions under schema `policy_questions.v1`: 15 for correctness, 7 for security, and 8 split across testing and architecture and maintainability, with the testing share confirmed at the sittings. AC-1, AC-2.
- R2. Every policy question carries exactly `id`, `lens`, `group`, `question`, `fails`, `clears`, `proving_test` and `not_for`, all non-empty, with `not_for` a list of at least one line. The wording is public-safe: no committed text names a private repository. AC-2.

**The four banks**

- R3. Four bank files hold schema `question_bank.v1` with 10 to 20 questions each, every question's `lens` equal to its file's lens, and question ids unique across all four banks. AC-1, AC-3.
- R4. Every bank question carries options with definitions, at least one example and at least one not-for line. A yes-no question orders its options `yes` then `no`. A choice question has at least two options, and the first option is the one whose probability fires. AC-3.
- R5. Every question folded from an optional lens names its source lens in its id and carries a non-empty switch-on condition in C6's `when` format. All eight folded sources appear in their target banks, with deployment and infrastructure in both security and architecture. AC-4.
- R6. No bank question targets an input the lens tables give to a tool or a check. The test holds the exclusion as a fixed stem list derived row by row from the lens tables. AC-5.
- R7. No bank text names a private repository. The committed deny-list holds only names already public in the card and issue; leakage of names that cannot be committed is checked at the sittings against the private draft. AC-2, AC-3.

**The loader and the approver**

- R8. `plugins/saga/scripts/question_banks.py` validates shape, loads approved sets and writes approvals. `load` refuses a lens whose content differs from its approval or that carries none, with empty standard output and exit 2. `approve` writes the approval date and fingerprint. `--help` exits 0. AC-7, AC-8.
- R9. Every shipped bank's approval matches its current content, and the test fails while any shipped set differs from its approval. AC-7.

**Sittings and fingerprint**

- R10. Each lens has one sitting covering its policy questions, its Jev questions with what each is for, the C5 and C5b outcomes and the C4a to C4c mappings and curated lists for that lens, with examples from the corpus tuning half where the corpus has them. Each sitting is noted on issue #157 with its date. AC-6.
- R11. Each bank passes the real sweep parser: `jev sweep` with that bank and an empty piece list exits 0 without reaching the network. AC-1, AC-3.
- R12. C2's fingerprinted component list names the five JSON files and the loader, in the module, in the reference fence and in the equality test. AC-9.

## Key Technical Decisions

- KTD1. The layout follows the card's proposal: `plugins/saga/references/question-banks/` holds `policy-questions.json` plus `security.json`, `correctness.json`, `testing.json` and `architecture-maintainability.json`, beside `plugins/saga/scripts/question_banks.py`. JSON keeps the banks readable by fleet-core's standard library when saga hands one to the sweep. Rejected: YAML, which the sweep cannot read without leaving the standard library.
- KTD2. Each bank file is an envelope the sweep accepts untouched: `schema`, `lens`, `approval`, `policy` and `questions` at the top level. Both C6 readers take only `schema` and `questions` and never enumerate top-level keys, while per-question keys are closed, so top-level metadata cannot break the sweep and per-question metadata cannot exist. The fingerprint is the sha256 of the canonical JSON of `{"bank": <file minus approval>, "policy": <this lens's policy entries in file order>}`.
- KTD3. A folded question's source lens rides in its question id as `<lens>.<source>.<slug>`, and a bank question drawn from a policy question names that policy id in the file's top-level `policy` map. Ids travel into sweep items, verdict logs and calibration thresholds, so the source lens keeps its provenance end to end, while the map joins banks to policy without bloating ids. Rejected: per-question `source` and `policy` keys, which the sweep's closed key set refuses, and a sidecar file, which could drift from the bank it describes.
- KTD4. The loader refuses on shape plus approval only: malformed JSON, a C6 rule broken, an unknown policy link, a missing approval or a fingerprint mismatch all exit 2 with empty standard output, mirroring the review runner's refusal code. Content rules (counts, folded coverage, tool exclusion, deny-list) are test assertions, frozen after the fact by the approval. `approve` runs only on the operator's word as noted on the issue; that gate is process, verified by reading the issue, not a check the command can perform.
- KTD5. Folded switch-on conditions stay broad until the corpus narrows them. A folded question whose kind of code has no safe static boundary carries `when.languages` as the full review-language list: explicit, operator-visible at the sitting, and tunable by K3. Rejected: guessing narrow path patterns now, which is exactly the quiet gap the card names as its top risk.
- KTD6. The sittings are the last unit and wait for C4c, C5 and C5b to land, because AC-6 needs their mappings and outcomes on the table. Sitting material is rendered by `question_banks.py sitting --lens`, which pairs each question with its purpose derived from its policy link or source lens plus its examples, so no new schema field is needed. Tuning-half examples appear where K1 and K2 have produced them; otherwise the material uses written-fresh or public-history examples, and held-out cases never appear.
- KTD7. The committed deny-list holds only repository names already public in the card and issue, namely the issue's own example. Private names cannot be committed to a public repository even as test data, so the sitting, where the operator holds the private draft, is the check for the rest. The plan and the code never print a private name.
- KTD8. No version bump. Bumps in this repository ship only in `chore(release)` commits, the C6 plan set the precedent for leaving `plugin.json` alone, and no acceptance criterion asks for a new version. The Unreleased changelog is the record. This deliberately sets aside the card's `plugin.json` file listing.
- KTD9. Canonical JSON is `json.dumps` with sorted keys, compact separators, `ensure_ascii` off, encoded UTF-8. Approval dates are ISO 8601 calendar days. Both are pinned so the fingerprint is stable across machines and the test can recompute it by hand.
- KTD10. `question_banks.py` imports the standard library only, answers `--help` with no credentials in the environment, and is covered by the entrypoint discovery test without any change to that test.

## High-Level Technical Design

Content flows one way: the operator approves it at the sittings, `approve` fingerprints it, and `load` hands only approved bytes to the sweep and, later, to C11's declaration check.

A bank file:

```json
{
  "schema": "question_bank.v1",
  "lens": "security",
  "approval": {"date": "2026-10-09", "fingerprint": "<64 hex>"},
  "policy": {"security.caller-owns-record": "security-01"},
  "questions": [
    {
      "id": "security.caller-owns-record",
      "lens": "security",
      "kind": "yes-no",
      "options": [
        {"id": "yes", "definition": "..."},
        {"id": "no", "definition": "..."}
      ],
      "examples": ["..."],
      "not_for": ["..."],
      "when": {"languages": ["python", "typescript", "dart", "rust", "swift", "shell"]},
      "piece": "function"
    }
  ]
}
```

A policy entry:

```json
{
  "id": "security-01",
  "lens": "security",
  "group": "who may act on what",
  "question": "...",
  "fails": "...",
  "clears": "...",
  "proving_test": "...",
  "not_for": ["..."]
}
```

The eight folded sources land in their target banks as follows. Conditions are sketches; the implementer drafts them and the sitting confirms each one.

| Source lens | Target bank | Switch-on sketch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reliability | correctness | all code languages; no safe path boundary yet |
| api-contract | correctness | contract-bearing paths where safe, else all code languages |
| performance | correctness | all code languages; no safe path boundary yet |
| adversarial | security | all code languages; abuse needs no path |
| privacy | security | data-path patterns where safe, else all code languages |
| deployment-infrastructure | security, architecture | workflow and infrastructure paths plus code languages |
| documentation-clarity | architecture | documentation paths plus the code languages whose changes need docs |
| agent-usability | architecture | skill, command and tool paths plus code languages |

The loader's command surface is `load --lens` (sweep-ready bank JSON on standard output), `load --policy` (the policy list on standard output), `approve --lens [--date]` (rewrites the bank file, prints the fingerprint), and `sitting --lens` (Markdown sitting material on standard output). Refusals exit 2 with empty standard output and the reason on standard error; unexpected errors exit 1. The Python entry points mirror the commands for C11 to import.

The exclusion line for bank questions is mechanics versus substance. Forbidden is asking about the mechanics of proof: coverage, mutation survival, scanner outputs, type errors, test presence, collection, ordering, lint and format results, duplication and complexity numbers, and the rows C5 and C5b answer. Allowed and wanted is the substantive defect behind a policy question, such as whether a handler checks that the caller owns the record, which no tool answers. The two testing rows the lens tables answer by classifier then the reviewer always get questions.

## Implementation Units

### U1. The loader refuses and the approver records

The script exists with its full protocol before any real content does, proved against fixtures and temporary copies.

**Goal:** `question_banks.py` validates bank and policy shape, refuses unapproved or changed sets, writes approvals, renders sitting material, and answers `--help`.

**Requirements:** R8. KTD2, KTD4, KTD9, KTD10.

**Dependencies:** none.

**Files:**

- `plugins/saga/scripts/question_banks.py` (new)
- `plugins/saga/tests/test_question_banks.py` (new)

**Approach:** Split validation from approval. `validate_bank` and `validate_policy` check shape only: the schema tag, the exact key sets, option and `when` rules mirroring C6 strictness, lens agreement between file and questions, id shape (a C2-identifier starting with `<lens>.`, with any middle segment in the folded map for that lens), non-empty `when` on folded questions, and every `policy` map value resolving to a same-lens policy id. `load_bank` and `load_policy` validate, then require the approval block and recompute the KTD2 fingerprint over the shipped files. `approve` recomputes and writes `{"date", "fingerprint"}`. `sitting` renders Markdown pairing each question with its policy link or source lens, its options, and its examples. The CLI maps `load`, `approve` and `sitting` plus `--help`; refusals exit 2 with empty standard output.

**Patterns to follow:** `plugins/saga/scripts/review_calibration.py` for the refusal exception that exits 2, the repo-relative path handling, and the scripts-local stdlib-only shape. `plugins/saga/tests/test_entrypoints.py` for how discovery finds the new script.

**Test scenarios:**

- Happy path, `test_load_returns_the_sweep_shape_for_an_approved_bank`: a fixture bank with a correct approval loads to exactly `schema` plus `questions`, with no approval, lens tag or policy map in the output.
- Happy path, `test_approve_then_load_round_trips_on_temporary_copies`: approve a temporary bank copy, then load it; the returned content equals the file's questions and the printed fingerprint equals the stored one.
- Happy path, `test_approve_is_stable_byte_for_byte`: approving the same temporary content twice yields the same fingerprint and the same file bytes.
- Happy path, `test_sitting_pairs_each_question_with_its_purpose`: the rendered Markdown names every fixture question id beside its policy link or source lens.
- Error path, `test_load_refuses_a_bank_whose_question_changed`: a temporary copy with one edited definition refuses, and the CLI exits 2 with empty standard output.
- Error path, `test_load_refuses_a_bank_with_no_approval`: the approval block deleted from a temporary copy refuses.
- Error path, `test_load_refuses_a_changed_policy_entry`: a temporary policy copy with one edited `fails` line refuses the lens that cites it.
- Error path, `test_load_refuses_a_question_with_an_unexpected_key`: a ninth per-question key refuses, so the loader never hands the sweep a bank the sweep would reject.
- Error path, `test_load_refuses_a_folded_id_with_an_unknown_source`: a three-part id whose middle segment is not a folded source for that lens refuses.
- Error path, `test_load_refuses_a_policy_link_to_an_unknown_id`: a `policy` map value naming no policy question refuses, as does a value naming another lens's question.
- Edge case, `test_help_exits_zero_without_credentials`: the `question_banks.py --help` subprocess exits 0 with the credential prefixes stripped from the environment.
- Integration, `test_entrypoint_discovery_covers_the_new_script`: the entrypoint suite collects the new script and its `--help` passes through the existing discovery.

**Verification:** The loader tests pass against fixtures only; no shipped content is needed. The `--help` subprocess exits 0, and the entrypoint discovery test names the new script.

## System-Wide Impact

Six new fingerprinted components mean every later edit to a bank, the policy list or the loader needs a fresh corpus run before it merges, once a run is recorded. Until then the bytes are unchecked and only the approval tests guard the content.

The loader is C11's future import for handing banks to the sweep and the policy list to builders. Its refusal contract (exit 2, empty standard output) is the shape C11 must handle: a refused lens cannot silently become an empty bank.

Bank question ids flow into sweep items, verdict logs and calibration thresholds, so the `<lens>.<source>.<slug>` convention becomes long-lived provenance. Renaming a bank question later orphans its history; the approval fingerprint makes that rename visible, not free.

## Risks & Dependencies

| Risk | What makes it smaller |
| --- | --- |
| A gap in the banks misses defects quietly | Switch-on conditions stay broad until the corpus narrows them; the operator reviews every question at the sittings; every lens reports only until the calibration file shows it cleared its marks |
| Sittings cannot run before C4c, C5 and C5b land | They are the last unit with an explicit gate; the loader, policy list and banks land first and wait approved-by-test but unapproved-by-operator |
| Drafted wording drifts from the private policy draft | The operator, who holds the draft, corrects wording at the sittings; the implementer drafts only from the public lens tables and named topics |
| A private example or name leaks into public text | Fresh or public-history examples only; the committed deny-list test; the sitting as the check for names that cannot be committed |
| Approval churn on every late content edit | `approve` is one command per lens; the test fails loudly while stale, which is the enforcement working as designed |
| C6's bank format drifts under the loader | The loader mirrors C6 strictness, and the sweep-parser subprocess test fails on the first divergence |

Depends on C6, C2, C4a and C4b, all landed on this branch. Waits for C4c, C5 and C5b for the sittings only. K1 to K3 supply tuning-half examples where they exist and later tuning. C8, C10a, C11 and C15 consume this card's files and wait for it.
